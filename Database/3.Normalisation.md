# Database Normalization in DBMS

## Overview
Database normalization is a process of organizing data in a database to reduce redundancy and improve data integrity. It involves decomposing tables into smaller, related tables and defining relationships between them.

---

## Forms of Normalization

### **1. First Normal Form (1NF)**
**Rule:** Remove duplicate columns and ensure atomic values (indivisible values).

**Example - Before 1NF:**
| StudentID | Name | Courses |
|-----------|------|---------|
| 1 | John | Math, Physics, Chemistry |
| 2 | Sarah | English, History |

**After 1NF:**
| StudentID | Name | Course |
|-----------|------|--------|
| 1 | John | Math |
| 1 | John | Physics |
| 1 | John | Chemistry |
| 2 | Sarah | English |
| 2 | Sarah | History |

---

### **2. Second Normal Form (2NF)**
**Rules:** Must be in 1NF AND remove partial dependencies (non-key attributes must depend on the entire primary key).

**Example - Before 2NF:**
| StudentID | CourseID | StudentName | CourseName |
|-----------|----------|-------------|-----------|
| 1 | 101 | John | Math |
| 1 | 102 | John | Physics |

**After 2NF:**

**Students Table:**
| StudentID | StudentName |
|-----------|-------------|
| 1 | John |
| 2 | Sarah |

**Courses Table:**
| CourseID | CourseName |
|----------|-----------|
| 101 | Math |
| 102 | Physics |

**Enrollment Table:**
| StudentID | CourseID |
|-----------|----------|
| 1 | 101 |
| 1 | 102 |

---

### **3. Third Normal Form (3NF)**
**Rules:** Must be in 2NF AND remove transitive dependencies (non-key attributes must not depend on other non-key attributes).

**Example - Before 3NF:**
| EmployeeID | EmployeeName | DepartmentID | DepartmentName | Manager |
|-----------|-------------|--------------|----------------|---------|
| 101 | Alice | D1 | Sales | Bob |
| 102 | Charlie | D1 | Sales | Bob |

**After 3NF:**

**Employees Table:**
| EmployeeID | EmployeeName | DepartmentID |
|-----------|-------------|--------------|
| 101 | Alice | D1 |
| 102 | Charlie | D1 |

**Departments Table:**
| DepartmentID | DepartmentName | Manager |
|--------------|----------------|---------|
| D1 | Sales | Bob |

---

### **4. Boyce-Codd Normal Form (BCNF)**
**Rule:** Must be in 3NF AND for every functional dependency, the determinant must be a candidate key.

**Example - Before BCNF:**
| Professor | Course | Time |
|-----------|--------|------|
| Dr. Smith | Math | 9 AM |
| Dr. Smith | Physics | 10 AM |
| Dr. Jones | Math | 11 AM |

**After BCNF:**

**Professor_Course Table:**
| Professor | Course |
|-----------|--------|
| Dr. Smith | Math |
| Dr. Smith | Physics |
| Dr. Jones | Math |

**Course_Schedule Table:**
| Course | Time |
|--------|------|
| Math | 9 AM |
| Physics | 10 AM |

---

### **5. Fourth Normal Form (4NF)**
**Rule:** Must be in BCNF AND have no non-trivial multivalued dependencies.

---

### **6. Fifth Normal Form (5NF) - Project-Join Normal Form**
**Rule:** Must be in 4NF AND have no join dependencies that cannot be reconstructed from smaller tables.

**Example - Before 5NF:**
| Instructor | Course | Time |
|-----------|--------|------|
| Dr. Smith | Math | 9 AM |
| Dr. Smith | Physics | 10 AM |
| Dr. Jones | Math | 11 AM |
| Dr. Jones | Physics | 2 PM |

**After 5NF:**

**Instructor_Course Table:**
| Instructor | Course |
|-----------|--------|
| Dr. Smith | Math |
| Dr. Smith | Physics |
| Dr. Jones | Math |
| Dr. Jones | Physics |

**Course_Time Table:**
| Course | Time |
|--------|------|
| Math | 9 AM |
| Physics | 10 AM |

**Instructor_Time Table:**
| Instructor | Time |
|-----------|------|
| Dr. Smith | 9 AM |
| Dr. Smith | 10 AM |
| Dr. Jones | 11 AM |
| Dr. Jones | 2 PM |

---

### **7. Sixth Normal Form (6NF) - Temporal Normal Form**
**Rule:** Ensure temporal consistency by separating time-dependent attributes from time-independent ones.

**Example - Before 6NF:**
| EmployeeID | EmployeeName | Salary | Position | ValidFrom | ValidTo |
|-----------|-------------|--------|----------|-----------|---------|
| 101 | John | 50000 | Developer | 2024-01-01 | 2025-06-30 |
| 101 | John | 60000 | Senior Dev | 2025-07-01 | 2026-06-15 |
| 102 | Sarah | 55000 | Analyst | 2024-06-01 | 2026-06-15 |

**After 6NF:**

**Employees Table:**
| EmployeeID | EmployeeName |
|-----------|-------------|
| 101 | John |
| 102 | Sarah |

**Employee_Salary_History Table:**
| EmployeeID | Salary | ValidFrom | ValidTo |
|-----------|--------|-----------|---------|
| 101 | 50000 | 2024-01-01 | 2025-06-30 |
| 101 | 60000 | 2025-07-01 | 2026-06-15 |
| 102 | 55000 | 2024-06-01 | 2026-06-15 |

**Employee_Position_History Table:**
| EmployeeID | Position | ValidFrom | ValidTo |
|-----------|----------|-----------|---------|
| 101 | Developer | 2024-01-01 | 2025-06-30 |
| 101 | Senior Dev | 2025-07-01 | 2026-06-15 |
| 102 | Analyst | 2024-06-01 | 2026-06-15 |

## Benefits of Normalization
✓ Reduces data redundancy  
✓ Improves data consistency  
✓ Minimizes update anomalies  
✓ Enhances query performance  
✓ Ensures referential integrity
